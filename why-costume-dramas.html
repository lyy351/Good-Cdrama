A Short Explainer

Why Many Chinese Viewers Are Unhappy with Costume Dramas

为什么中国有相当一部分人对现在的古装剧非常不满意

If you're just getting into Chinese dramas and everyone around you keeps complaining about "how bad costume dramas have become," this page is for you. It's not about taste — it's about a set of structural problems that a large portion of Chinese viewers can feel but rarely articulate in one place.

如果你刚开始看中剧，却发现身边一直有人在抱怨“古装剧越来越难看了”，这篇就是给你看的。它不讲口味，而是讲一大群中国观众能感受到、但很少被一次性说清楚的结构性问题。

1. Bloated Episode Counts集数注水

A story that could be told in 24 episodes gets stretched to 50 or 60. Subplots get added, flashbacks get recycled, and pacing dies. The reason is partly commercial: broadcasters and platforms historically paid by the episode, so longer shows meant more revenue. Streaming has started to change this, but the habit is baked in.

一个 24 集就能讲完的故事，被拉到 50 甚至 60 集。副线加戏、回忆反复用、节奏拖死。原因一部分是商业逻辑：过去电视台和平台按集数买剧，集数越多收入越高。流媒体时代开始改变这一点，但惯性还在。

2. Weak Scripts, Recycled Tropes剧本薄弱 · 套路重复

Many costume dramas are adapted from web novels, and the adaptation process often strips out what made the original interesting — the moral ambiguity, the slow character work, the difficult choices — and replaces it with market-tested beats. The result is shows that feel like they were assembled from a checklist.

很多古装剧改编自网络小说，而改编过程常常把原作最有意思的部分——道德的模糊、人物的缓慢成长、艰难的选择——删掉，换成经过市场验证的桥段。结果就是很多剧像是照着清单拼出来的。

3. Over-Filtered Visuals滤镜过重

Modern Chinese costume dramas are often graded to within an inch of their life — skin textures erased, colors pushed toward pastel, nighttime scenes bright as day. This is partly about star images (actors' faces must look flawless) and partly about trying to make the show feel "premium." Many viewers find it exhausting and, ironically, cheap-looking.

现在的古装剧经常磨皮磨到没有皮肤纹理、色调全部偏马卡龙、夜景亮如白昼。一部分原因是偶像形象管理（演员的脸必须完美），一部分是想让剧显得“高级”。很多观众反而觉得这种画面很累、很廉价。

4. Dubbing Mismatch配音违和

Many stars are dubbed by professional voice actors. If you don't know the language, you may not notice. If you do, it can feel like watching a puppet. It also contributes to a sense that the performer on screen isn't fully inhabiting the role.

很多明星由专业配音演员配音。不懂中文的观众可能听不出来，但听得出来的人会觉得像在看木偶戏。这也让人感觉演员没有完全进入角色。

5. Same Faces, Same Genres题材同质化 · 熟脸循环

Immortal romance, palace intrigue, revenge arcs, "strong female lead" that turns out to be a marketing label. The same dozen actors rotate through the same dozen story shapes. Original concepts get greenlit less often because proven formulas are safer.

仙侠恋爱、宫斗、复仇、“大女主”——但所谓“大女主”往往只是营销标签。同一批演员在同一批故事模板里轮流转。真正原创的题材更难立项，因为成熟公式更安全。

6. Traffic Stars Over Actors重流量 · 轻演技

Casting is often driven by fanbase size rather than suitability. "Traffic stars" — actors with massive online followings — can carry a show's marketing even if they can't carry its emotional weight. This isn't universal, but it's common enough that viewers have learned to be wary the moment a certain name is attached.

选角常常看粉丝体量而不是适配度。“流量明星”——拥有庞大网络粉丝的演员——即使撑不起情感分量，也能撑起剧的宣传。这不是普遍现象，但足够常见，常见到观众一看到某个名字就开始警惕。

7. Costume and Set Inconsistency服化道不考究

Some shows invest heavily in historically informed design. Many don't. Mixed dynasties, anachronistic fabrics, modern makeup on ancient characters. For viewers who care about this, it breaks immersion. It also signals a certain indifference to the material.

有些剧在服化道上投入很大、考据严谨。但很多剧不是。混搭朝代、面料穿越、古人化现代妆。在乎这些的观众会瞬间出戏。它也反映出制作方对题材本身的某种不尊重。

8. Values That Feel Regressive价值观争议

A show can be beautifully made and still be criticized for celebrating things like blind loyalty, glorified sacrifice, abusive dynamics framed as romance, or the idea that suffering is inherently noble. Chinese audiences are not monolithic — some love these, some are exhausted by them.

一部剧可以拍得很美，但仍然会因为赞美盲目的忠诚、被美化的牺牲、被包装成爱情的虐待关系、或者“受苦本身就是高尚”这类观念而被批评。中国观众不是铁板一块——有人爱这些，也有人看累了。

9. Censorship Pressure审查压力

Certain themes are simply off-limits or must be softened. This isn't a topic I want to overstate, but it's real, and it shapes what stories can be told and how. Sometimes the compromise is invisible; sometimes it leaves a hole in the middle of the plot that no one can explain.

有些题材直接被禁止或必须弱化。这个话题我不想夸大到所有问题上，但它是真实存在的，而且影响了哪些故事可以被讲、怎么讲。有时候这种妥协看不出来；有时候它就在剧情正中间留下一块谁也解释不了的空白。

10. The Fan Economy Bubble粉丝经济泡沫

Some shows are made first for fandoms and second for general audiences. Marketing focuses on shipping, fan service, and manufactured moments rather than on the story. This can work commercially, but it also creates an ecosystem where the loudest voices and the most general viewers want completely different things — and the shows increasingly cater to the former.

有些剧首先是为粉丝做的，其次才是给普通观众的。营销重点放在 CP 感、粉丝福利、人造名场面上，而不是故事本身。这在商业上可能成立，但也造成一种生态：声量最大的群体和普通观众想要的东西完全不同——而剧越来越迎合前者。

Closing Thought结语

None of this means every recent costume drama is bad, or that older shows were all better. It means the dissatisfaction many Chinese viewers feel isn't just nostalgia. It's a reaction to a set of concrete, overlapping problems that make watching a costume drama feel, more often than not, like an exercise in lowering expectations.

这并不是说近年所有古装剧都很差，也不是说老剧都好。它只是说明：很多中国观众的不满不只是怀旧。它是对一整套具体且相互叠加的问题的反应——这套问题让看古装剧这件事，越来越像在练习降低期望。

If you want to see what Chinese costume dramas can be when these problems are set aside, look at Nirvana in Fire, The Longest Day in Chang'an, The Story of Minglan.
如果你想看这些问题被暂时放下时古装剧能达到什么水准，可以去看《琅琊榜》《长安十二时辰》《知否知否应是绿肥红瘦》。
